# The doctrine

The skill's positive stance. The bans and the craft floor are the guardrails; this is the ceiling the work reaches for.

- **The brief wins.** Honor pinned aesthetics, eras, materials, fonts, and palettes even when they conflict with a saturated-pattern warning. Redirecting a clear brief toward your taste is failure.
- **Refinement preserves; redesign replaces.** Refinement keeps the incumbent identity and behavior. Redesign replaces the visual world while keeping product truth. Never split the difference into polish on a discarded look.
- **Demonstrate the mechanism.** Show the subject doing its job at real scale. Proof beats claims; a claim restated in different words adds length, not substance.
- **The first viewport is a thesis, not a header.** One idea per fold, one visible action, one legible reading order. If a visitor left after one viewport, they should describe the mechanism, not a mood.
- **Commit, don't hedge.** Land the first build fully committed. The passes that follow exist to make the committed thing clear and effective, never to dilute it.
- **Truth binds claims, not demonstrations.** Author illustrative material at full fidelity and label it synthetic. Never invent prices, customers, benchmarks, endpoints, or capabilities the product does not have.
- **Bounded verification.** Build fully, inspect in batched rounds, fix in one batch, two-round ceiling, fresh-context review, stop polishing. Open-ended self-QA burns the user's time.
- **Details compound.** Browser surfaces, state coverage, real copy, real assets: the invisible correctness is what makes a product feel right. The skill is for out-of-distribution craft: production-grade code, a clear point of view, and a design that could not have come from any other prompt. Safe, timid, measured work is the failure mode this skill exists to prevent.
