# Landing page playbook (Persuade)

A landing page is a story with five beats. Structure follows the narrative; design follows each beat's job. The beats are the section order, but not every beat needs its own section: a short landing compresses Problem and Guide into one section, a long one expands Proof across testimonials, demos, and numbers. What must not change is the order and the escalation from empathy to proof to action.

## The five beats

1. **Hook (hero):** grab attention instantly with a headline that speaks to the core user desire or main pain point. One line, one action, one reading order. See [hero.md](hero.md).
2. **The Problem (conflict):** name the struggle the user faces so they feel understood and think "this is for me". The page earns empathy before it asks for belief.
3. **The Guide (product):** introduce the solution not as a list of features but as a helpful tool that makes life easier. Feature lists prove nothing; the mechanism does. Demonstrate, don't describe.
4. **The Proof (rising action):** social proof, testimonials, or short demos build trust and eliminate doubt. Real content only: real quotes, real numbers, real results. Decorative stats are banned.
5. **The Resolution (call to action):** end by telling the user exactly what step to take next. One verb, one action, one path.

## Per-beat checks

- **Hook:** the memory test (what would a visitor describe an hour later?), the offer lands in one line, the primary action is visible.
- **Problem:** named in the user's words, not the category's. A visitor should recognize their own situation on sight.
- **Guide:** the mechanism demonstrated at real scale, or the interface at work. Feature lists collapse into chrome.
- **Proof:** every proof item attributable: who said it, what result. Testimonials without a source are decoration.
- **Resolution:** one primary CTA, repeated at most once more; the form is short; the action names the outcome ("Start free", "See the result").

## Ruts per beat

- **Hook ruts:** hero-metric, centered stack, eyebrow pill, the library hero. See [hero.md](hero.md).
- **Problem ruts:** the generic pain ("Are you tired of X?" with no specifics), or the beat skipped entirely straight into features.
- **Guide ruts:** the feature grid (icon plus heading plus text, repeated), the "X that does Y" label with no demonstration.
- **Proof ruts:** fake metrics, logos without names, testimonials without attribution.
- **Resolution ruts:** the CTA buried, two competing primary CTAs, "Get started" with no stated outcome.
