## A. What makes AI-generated layouts recognizable (13 patterns beyond the existing 26 rules)

1. **Mode collapse / distributional convergence.** LLMs return to the center of their training distribution (Tailwind defaults, shadcn/ui, Vercel templates), so un-directed prompts converge on one aesthetic. Source: https://sailop.com/blog/why-every-ai-generated-website-looks-the-same [VENDOR] measured specifics (Inter in 73% of AI frontends; blue-500 #3b82f6; rounded-lg; shadow-md; transition-all duration-300); corroborated by https://medium.com/@dminhk/why-do-ai-generated-websites-all-look-identical-02a68015613d [ESSAY] coins "distributional convergence."
2. **Uniform symmetric composition / equal-weight everything.** Three equal cards, equal widths, equal paddings, every feature reading as important as every other — a hierarchy nobody decided. Real content is lopsided because real features are not equally important. Source: https://uxskill.laithjunaidy.com/how-to-tell-if-a-website-was-ai-generated.html [VENDOR] open-source MIT linter author, 9-sign ranked checklist; corroborated by https://sailop.com/blog/card-grid-problem-why-3-identical-cards-kill-conversion [VENDOR].
3. **Centered-stack default.** Headline, subhead, buttons, body paragraphs all centered on the centerline; "if your eye never has to move left or right, that is the pattern." Confident design uses an offset grid or strong left edge. Source: https://uxskill.laithjunaidy.com/how-to-tell-if-a-website-was-ai-generated.html [VENDOR]; also https://rottoways.com/blog/vibecoding-design-problem [VENDOR] pattern #4 (centered `max-w-2xl mx-auto` body copy) and https://sailop.com/blog/why-every-ai-generated-website-looks-the-same [VENDOR] (the 100vh centered-hero template).
4. **Identical section rhythm.** One template spine: Navbar > Hero > Features > Testimonials > CTA > Footer, in that order, every time, with identical top/bottom padding per section. Source: https://sailop.com/blog/what-is-ai-slop-7-dimensions-of-generic-design [VENDOR] Dimension 6 (structure) and Dimension 3 (section-padding variation).
5. **Generic 8px-grid compliance without art direction.** Every spacing/gap a multiple of 4 or 8 (p-4/p-8/p-12), every container `max-w-7xl`; mechanical grid adherence reads as machine-stamped, whereas intentional design breaks the grid for rhythm. Source: https://sailop.com/blog/what-is-ai-slop-7-dimensions-of-generic-design [VENDOR] Dimension 7; counter-technique in https://www.fonts.com/content/learning/fontology/level-2/display-typography/visual-alignment [PRIMARY] (type set by eye, not by number).
6. **No negative-space strategy.** Soft shadows + uniform rounding on every surface produce a floating, weightless page with no focal point; nothing is flat, nothing has a hard edge. Source: https://uxskill.laithjunaidy.com/how-to-tell-if-a-website-was-ai-generated.html [VENDOR] sign #7; mechanism (RLHF rewards "safe" = uniform over "interesting" = contrast) in https://rottoways.com/blog/vibecoding-design-problem [VENDOR].
7. **Template heroes.** Hero = centered headline, 2-line subtitle, primary+ghost button, dot-grid/gradient backdrop, pinned to 100vh. "The pattern isn't bad. It's just the only one the model reaches for." Source: https://sailop.com/blog/why-every-ai-generated-website-looks-the-same [VENDOR]; corroborated by https://me.pcmag.com/en/ai/29714/i-review-website-builders-for-a-living-heres-4-reasons-ai-is-bad-at-making-sites [PRIMARY REVIEW] PCMag reviewer, cookie-cutter results across builders.
8. **Safe typography pairing (new "Claude Design" cliché).** Fraunces+Inter, one italic serif word carrying the emotional weight, dark background, muted violet/amber accents, monospace labels in card corners, "Preview/Coming Soon" pill — a refined but instantly recognizable house style. Source: https://theaicliff.substack.com/p/ai-design-already-has-a-cliche-youre [ESSAY] agency veteran turned AI engineer, Apr 2026; corroborated by https://www.ibtimes.sg/why-so-many-ai-built-websites-are-starting-look-same-91292 [ESSAY] trade coverage of the same "Claude Design" phenomenon.
9. **Generic icon usage: tinted circles and stock glyphs.** Lucide check icons inside `bg-emerald-500/10` circles in every feature/pricing list (a shadcn pricing-example default), plus Zap/Shield/Rocket glyphs everywhere. Source: https://rottoways.com/blog/vibecoding-design-problem [VENDOR] pattern #6; icon repertoire in https://sailop.com/blog/why-every-ai-generated-website-looks-the-same [VENDOR].
10. **Gradient "Most popular" pricing pill + three-tier pricing.** Middle plan lifted with gradient pill and gradient border, evenly spaced features with filler checklists. Source: https://rottoways.com/blog/vibecoding-design-problem [VENDOR] pattern #5; https://uxskill.laithjunaidy.com/how-to-tell-if-a-website-was-ai-generated.html [VENDOR] sign #5 (read the tier differences: if arbitrary, it was generated to fit the shape).
11. **Placeholder visual identity: DiceBear avatars, five-star rows, dark-scrim stock heroes.** Geometric avatar fallbacks, amber 5/5 star rows never tied to a real review, and scrimmed stock photos that could be swapped with zero loss. Sources: https://rottoways.com/blog/vibecoding-design-problem [VENDOR] patterns #7-#8; https://uxskill.laithjunaidy.com/how-to-tell-if-a-website-was-ai-generated.html [VENDOR] sign #6.
12. **Glassmorphism as shorthand for "modern."** `backdrop-blur-md` on every navbar, modal, and overlay; blur deployed as a status signal, not a legibility decision. Source: https://sailop.com/blog/why-every-ai-generated-website-looks-the-same [VENDOR] ("glassmorphism is an AI tell" section).
13. **No editorial contrast.** One typeface doing every job (headline font == paragraph font), line-height 1.5 everywhere, weight 700 on all headings, identical motion on every element — zero contrast between display and body. Sources: https://uxskill.laithjunaidy.com/how-to-tell-if-a-website-was-ai-generated.html [VENDOR] sign #3; https://rottoways.com/blog/vibecoding-design-problem [VENDOR] pattern #3 (Inter 700 display); https://sailop.com/blog/what-is-ai-slop-7-dimensions-of-generic-design [VENDOR] Dimension 2/4.

## B. Dashboard / complex-UX layout norms (13 concrete rules with numbers)

1. **Data-table row heights are a defined scale, not freeform.** Carbon: xs 24px, sm 32px, md 40px, lg 48px, xl 64px; the column-header row MUST match the body row height (never mix); xl only when cells need two lines. Source: https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/data-table/style/ [PRIMARY] IBM official spec.
2. **Table typography and padding.** Carbon: column headers 14px/600, row text 14px/400, 16px column padding, tables require >= 3 columns, sort-icon click target 32px, 48px toolbar pairs with lg/xl rows and 32px toolbar with sm/xs. Source: https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/data-table/style/ [PRIMARY].
3. **Density is a discrete scale with a 4px step.** Material: three levels (default 0, comfortable, compact -1/-2/-3); each compact step reduces component height by 4px without touching horizontal spacing; compact (-1..-3) is recommended for data-heavy layouts like tables. Source: https://medium.com/google-design/using-material-density-on-the-web-59d85f1918f0 [PRIMARY] Google Design, Una Kravets; density page https://m3.material.io/foundations/layout/grids-spacing/density [PRIMARY].
4. **Default data-grid row height ~52px.** MUI Data Grid defaults to 52px rows aligned to Material guidance; density variants adjust from there. Source: https://mui.com/x/react-data-grid/row-height/ [PRIMARY] maintained by MUI core team.
5. **Choose encodings humans perceive preattentively.** NN/g: length and 2D position (bars, scatter/line) support fast accurate comparison; area/angle (pie, donut, treemap) are poor for primary dashboard communication. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/ [PRIMARY] NN/g research-based article (Page Laubheimer).
6. **Color is a secondary cue, never the only one.** NN/g: use color to reinforce shape/proximity/grouping (Gestalt), and design for color-vision impairment. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/ [PRIMARY]; same rule for status indicators in Carbon (color+shape+icon+text label). Source: https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/status-indicator-pattern/ [PRIMARY].
7. **Information scent and density.** Dashboards should communicate the most important data succinctly; avoid overcrowding; linear, scannable visuals first. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/ [PRIMARY]; https://www.nngroup.com/articles/content-chrome-ratio/ [PRIMARY] maximize content-to-chrome ratio.
8. **Table task model.** NN/g: support four tasks — find records matching criteria, compare data, view/edit/add a row, act on records; human-readable first column as record identifier; order columns by relevance; freeze header + first column on long tables; zebra/hover aids. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/data-tables/ [PRIMARY].
9. **Mobile tables: filter first.** On narrow screens lock row headers, limit visible columns, and let users refine the dataset before display rather than shipping a wide multi-column table. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tables/ [PRIMARY].
10. **Navigation: visible, located, labeled.** NN/g menu checklist: keep nav visible on desktop (hamburger only when space demands), show current location, click-activate submenus with clear carets, avoid multi-level cascades, sticky nav on long pages. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/menu-design/ [PRIMARY].
11. **Forms: labels outside, fields sized to content.** NN/g: placeholders are harmful as label substitutes; labels above fields in single column; size inputs to expected content (city ~19 chars); group related fields; minimize optional fields. Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/ [PRIMARY]; https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders/ [PRIMARY]. Material 3 text fields standard height: 56px. Source: https://m3.material.io/components/text-fields/specs [PRIMARY].
12. **Dashboard composition hierarchy.** Carbon presentation dashboards: highest-importance data largest, highest contrast, top-left (F-pattern); limit the number of metrics; consistent color mapping per dataset; whitespace for clarity. Exploration dashboards: linked charts, consistent legends, annotations that don't obstruct data. Source: https://carbondesignsystem.com/data-visualization/dashboards/ [PRIMARY].
13. **Grid behavior by interface density.** Carbon 2x grid: high-density interfaces (dashboards, catalogs) go full-width with condensed gutters; editorial/marketing centers content; product/docs uses left nav + fixed max width. Source: https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/2x-grid/usage/ [PRIMARY]. USWDS adds: striped/scrollable/stacked table variants, compact variant for dense numeric data, monospace for numbers, minimize columns, sticky headers. Source: https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/table/ [PRIMARY] U.S. federal design system.

## C. Editorial / non-AI layout techniques (11 techniques)

1. **The grid is a mental attitude, not a decoration.** Müller-Brockmann: the grid expresses "the will to systematize, to clarify" and "to cultivate objectivity"; systematic arrangement makes priorities stand out. Source: https://www.neugraphic.com/muller-brockmann/muller-brockmann-text2.html [PRIMARY] verbatim republication of Grid Systems in Graphic Design (1981) text.
2. **Derive the grid from the content.** "Every piece of work must be studied very carefully so as to arrive at the specific grid network corresponding to its requirements" — the grid encodes content relationships, which is why AI's one-size-fits-all grids fail. Source: https://www.neugraphic.com/muller-brockmann/muller-brockmann-text2.html [PRIMARY].
3. **Fewer element sizes = quieter design.** "The fewer the differences in the size of the illustrations, the quieter the impression" — restraint is a deliberate choice, distinct from AI's undifferentiated sameness. Source: https://www.neugraphic.com/muller-brockmann/muller-brockmann-text2.html [PRIMARY].
4. **Asymmetric editorial grids.** 5-9 columns with gutters sized to text; asymmetry is a feature, and breakouts (pull quotes, dominant images) read as deliberate only when the underlying grid is strong; baseline must align text, images, captions. Source: https://www.affinity.studio/blog/grid-design-for-editorial-and-web-layouts [ESSAY] design studio writeup; http://www.fonts.com/content/learning/fontology/level-2/display-typography/visual-alignment [PRIMARY] Fonts.com typography learning.
5. **Content-first, typography-driven layout.** Editorial web design starts from words: viewport-scaled display type, two type families for hierarchy, visible multi-column grid, offset imagery, whitespace as structure framing the type. Source: https://vikilinks.com/blog/editorial-web-design [ESSAY] practical guide.
6. **One focal element per screen + intentional asymmetry.** Move away from repetitive card grids: staggered columns, varied line lengths, one focal point with the rest responding; asymmetry must serve the communication goal, not chaos. Source: https://lab.daembrace.com/2026/03/03/the-return-of-editorial-layouts-in-digital-design/ [ESSAY] design lab analysis.
7. **Optical alignment over mechanical alignment.** Mechanical leading and geometric centering do not produce visually even spacing; adjust display type by eye (cap-height lines, ascenders/descenders, optical centering of icons inside shapes). Sources: http://www.fonts.com/content/learning/fontology/level-2/display-typography/visual-alignment [PRIMARY]; https://design.liferay.com/articles/best-practices/measured-vs-optical-alignment/ [PRIMARY] Liferay design docs.
8. **Baseline grids for rhythm.** Align all text to a shared baseline (classic Swiss practice, implementable on the web with an 8px baseline and runtime optical-alignment verification). Source: https://github.com/voltwake/grid-typesetting-systems [PRIMARY] working, verifiable implementation (0px-adherence verifier); theory at https://medium.com/@gombau/the-baseline-grid-friend-or-foe-d4bec9ae595e [ESSAY].
9. **Respect the measure: 45-75 characters per line.** ~66 chars ideal for single-column; multi-column ~40-50; the measure, not the container, drives readability. Sources: https://webtypography.net/2.1.2 [PRIMARY] Bringhurst's Elements of Typographic Style applied to web; https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability [PRIMARY] Baymard research synthesis (50-75).
10. **Hierarchy via contrast in scale, weight, color.** Build a scale from body text with a consistent multiplier (1.5/1.618/2.0) and differentiate tiers by weight, not size alone; exaggerated scale contrast (huge headline, small caption) creates editorial energy. Sources: https://fiveable.me/advanced-editorial-design/unit-3/typographic-hierarchy-scale/study-guide/B1cAgPiV1GWWeXc3 [ESSAY] editorial-design curriculum; https://www.mediapoint.com.au/design-tips/typography-techniques-print-design [ESSAY] print-production guide.
11. **Tufte density: maximize data-ink.** data-ink ratio = data-ink / total ink; erase non-data ink, keep high data density (entries per area) so information displays earn their space — the normative counter to both sparse AI marketing pages and chartjunk. Source: https://www.edwardtufte.com/book/the-visual-display-of-quantitative-information/ [PRIMARY] the primary book; formula notes at https://cjlm.ca/notes/the-visual-display-of-quantitative-information/ [ESSAY].

## D. What AI design tools demonstrably get wrong (7 findings, 2024-2026)

1. **Neutral prompts produce deceptive designs, without warnings.** CHI 2025 study (20 participants, GPT-4o): every generated webshop page contained >=1 deceptive pattern (mean 5, max 9); fake discounts, fake testimonials, countdown timers, pre-checked boxes, visual-prominence manipulations appeared from neutral business-goal prompts, and ChatGPT gave almost no warnings. Source: https://hcistudio.org/publication/krauss2025create/krauss2025create.pdf [PRIMARY] peer-reviewed CHI '25 paper (Krauß, McGill, et al.).
2. **Template-wrapped output across 12 builders.** Designer's controlled test (same cleaning-service brief, 12 tools): Wix "sterile," Squarespace "indistinguishable from every other template," 10Web "bland with low-quality images," Jimdo served a consulting headline to a cleaning company, Readdy's AI images "uncanny," Bolt too bare; only Framer/Lovable approached designed quality. Source: https://icons8.com/blog/articles/ai-website-builder-test/ [PRIMARY REVIEW] Icons8 designer, dated test with screenshots.
3. **Fidelity failures even from a reference screenshot.** Lovable/Bolt/v0 comparisons: misaligned elements, missing or misplaced icons, inconsistent styling, non-functional buttons, incomplete screens; none reproduced the reference accurately, and vague prompts made all three converge on the same generic output. Source: https://techpoint.africa/guide/lovable-vs-bolt-v0-review/ [PRIMARY REVIEW] tool-by-tool screenshot fidelity test; https://www.epam.com/insights/ai/blogs/best-vibe-coding-tools-v0-lovable-bolt-replit-and-figma-make [PRIMARY REVIEW] EPAM engineering review, same conclusion.
4. **Same prompt, same design, three different tools.** Bolt, Lovable, and Replit produced near-identical sites from one prompt — direct evidence that sameness lives in the model corpus, not the tool. Source: https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/i-asked-3-ai-tools-to-design-a-website-and-they-all-gave-me-the-same-one-82be1246dd7f [ESSAY] documented experiment, 2025.
5. **Functional promises break at the edges.** Lovable's "Book Now" CTA required manual backend setup, contradicting the zero-config promise; Bolt throws non-technical users into GitLab/Netlify integration. Source: https://icons8.com/blog/articles/ai-website-builder-test/ [PRIMARY REVIEW]; https://www.design-prism.com/blog/lovable-ai-review-business-owners [PRIMARY REVIEW] production-readiness testing.
6. **SEO/semantic structure overlooked.** Generated pages lack semantic HTML, schema, and proper heading hierarchy — AI optimizes the visible template, not the machine-readable structure that powers indexing and rich results. Source: https://axe-web.com/insights/ai-website-design-sameness/ [ESSAY] agency analysis of generated sites.
7. **The failure is structural, not prompt-fixable.** Tools are trained on a narrow corpus (shadcn/ui, Vercel templates, Tailwind docs, YC-startup pages) and RLHF ranks "safe" over "interesting," so switching tools keeps the same eight patterns; fixes must come from constraining the design system, not the prompt. Source: https://rottoways.com/blog/vibecoding-design-problem [VENDOR] technical breakdown; corroborated by https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website [ESSAY].

## Candidate new detector rules (16)

1. `triple_card_grid` — detects 3+ identical equal-width feature cards (`repeat(3,1fr)` / `grid-cols-3`) with icon+heading+2-line body. Extends: `icon_tile_stacks`, `nested_cards`. (A3, A9)
2. `centered_stack` — hero + body copy all centered on the same axis (no left edge, no offset column). Extends: `oversized_h1`. (A3)
3. `section_spine` — page section order matches Navbar>Hero>Features>Testimonials>CTA>Footer exactly. New detector (structure-level). (A4)
4. `uniform_section_padding` — identical vertical padding on every section (zero variation). New. (A4)
5. `strict_4px_grid` — every margin/padding/gap a multiple of 4 AND no off-grid values anywhere; penalize mechanical uniformity. New. (A5)
6. `max_width_default` — every content container is `max-w-7xl`/`max-w-6xl`. New. (A5)
7. `uniform_radius_scale` — one border-radius applied to every element class. Extends: `over_rounding`. (A6)
8. `uniform_motion` — `transition-all duration-300 ease-in-out` (or identical timing) on all interactive elements. Extends: `bounce_easing`. (A13)
9. `tinted_circle_icons` — icons inside soft tinted circles (e.g., `bg-emerald-500/10` + colored stroke). Extends: `emoji_icons` (generic icon usage). (A9)
10. `gradient_pricing_pill` — gradient "Most popular" badge on the middle pricing plan. Extends: `gradient_text`, `ai_palette`. (A10)
11. `five_star_rows` — 5-star amber rating rows on testimonials not linked to a review source. New. (A11)
12. `avatar_placeholder_url` — DiceBear/robohash/initials-generator avatar URLs in testimonial/social sections. Extends: `broken_images`. (A11)
13. `glassmorphism_blur` — `backdrop-blur` on navbars/modals/overlays without a legibility purpose. New. (A12)
14. `mono_label_corner` — monospace micro-label in card corner + "Preview/Coming Soon" pills (Claude Design signature). Extends: `overused_fonts`. (A8)
15. `single_typeface_contrast` — headline font-family identical to body font-family (no display/body pairing). Extends: `overused_fonts`, `italic_serif_displays` (inverse). (A13, A8)
16. `heading_weight_uniform` — all headings weight 700 with no weight-tier differentiation. Extends: `crushed_tracking`. (A13)

## Candidate dashboard doctrine (10 normative rules)

1. Use a defined row-height scale (24/32/40/48/64px); header row height MUST match body row height; 64px only for two-line cells. (B1, B2)
2. Use discrete density levels (-1..-3 compact, 4px/step) for data-dense screens; never ad-hoc heights; drop below readability only with a documented reason. (B3)
3. Tables: >= 3 columns, 16px cell padding, human-readable first column, columns ordered by relevance, sticky header + frozen first column on scroll. (B2, B8)
4. Numbers: monospace alignment, right-aligned numeric columns, minimize column count, zebra/hover for row tracking. (B13, B8)
5. Charts: prefer length/2D-position encodings (bar, line, scatter); ban pie/donut/treemap as the primary quantitative encoding. (B5)
6. Color is never the sole encoding: pair with shape/position/text label; keep one color mapping per dataset across the dashboard. (B6)
7. Hierarchy: most important KPI top-left, largest, highest contrast; cap the metric count; whitespace between metric groups. (B12)
8. Forms: persistent labels outside fields (no placeholder-as-label), single column, fields sized to expected content (~19 chars city scale), grouped related fields. (B8, B11)
9. Navigation: persistent visible sidebar on desktop with current-location indicator; click-activated submenus; no multi-level cascades; sticky on long pages. (B10)
10. Density-aware grid: high-density screens may go full-width with condensed gutters; editorial content centers with a bounded measure (45-75 chars). (B13, C9)
